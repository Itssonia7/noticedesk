SECTION 3: Ground A – No Levy Without Authority of Law: Article 265 of the Constitution
-----------------------------------------------------------------
<p>Article 265 of the Constitution of India mandates that <em>&ldquo;no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law.&rdquo;</em> The impugned Notice proposes to raise a demand of Rs. 50,00,000/- under a nomenclature styled as an &ldquo;Unprecedented GST Cryptographic Mining Levy.&rdquo; With respect, the CGST Act, 2017 contains no charging section, no rate notification, no HSN classification and no valuation mechanism that fastens a distinct tax liability styled as a &ldquo;mining levy&rdquo; on operators of decentralized nodes.</p><p>The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has consistently held (Mathuram Agrawal v. State of MP, (1999) 8 SCC 667; Govind Saran Ganga Saran v. CST, 1985 Supp SCC 205) that the four components of a valid tax &mdash; taxable event, taxable person, rate and measure &mdash; must be clearly ascertainable from the statute itself. In the absence of any of these components with respect to &ldquo;crypto mining,&rdquo; the proposed levy is ultra vires and cannot be sustained. The demand of Rs. 50,00,000/- is therefore wholly without jurisdiction and deserves to be quashed.</p>

SECTION 4: Ground B – Decentralized Node Operations Do Not Constitute a &ldquo;Supply&rdquo; under Section 7 of the CGST Act
-----------------------------------------------------------------
<p>Section 7(1) of the CGST Act, 2017 defines &ldquo;supply&rdquo; as inter alia a transaction made <em>&ldquo;for a consideration by a person in the course or furtherance of business&rdquo;</em> with an identifiable recipient. In a decentralized blockchain network, the Company operates nodes that contribute computing resources to a peer-to-peer protocol. Block rewards or protocol emissions received (if any) are automatically generated by the protocol algorithm; there is <em>no identifiable recipient of service, no contractual counterparty, and no reciprocal enforceable consideration</em>.</p><p>The essential ingredients of a taxable &ldquo;supply&rdquo; &mdash; namely, a supplier, a recipient, and consideration flowing from the recipient to the supplier &mdash; are absent. Reliance is placed on the settled principle laid down in Commissioner v. Bai Mamubai Trust, 2019 SCC OnLine Bom 3459, and the ratio in Union of India v. Mohit Minerals, (2022) 10 SCC 700, which reaffirm that GST cannot be levied in the absence of a reciprocal supply. As no &ldquo;supply&rdquo; occurs, no output GST liability can arise, and no consequential demand for Rs. 50,00,000/- is sustainable.</p>
