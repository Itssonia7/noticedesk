SECTION 2: Statement of Facts and Nature of Refund Claim
-----------------------------------------------------------------
<p>The Applicant is a Private Limited Company duly registered under the CGST/MGST/IGST Acts, engaged, inter alia, in the export of goods (mobile phones and allied commodities). Exports have been effected on payment of IGST in terms of Section 16(3)(b) of the IGST Act, 2017, and refund of such IGST has been claimed as per the mechanism prescribed under Section 54 of the CGST Act read with Rule 96/89 of the CGST Rules, 2017.</p><p>The refund claim of Rs. 1,78,63,659/- for January 2026 was filed on 14.03.2026 vide ARN AA2703260810849. The impugned SCN in Form GST RFD-08 proposes to disallow refund aggregating Rs. 27,79,372/- on three narrow grounds: (a) alleged excess ITC of Rs. 3,51,274/- in GSTR-3B vis-à-vis GSTR-2B for January 2026; (b) alleged ineligible ITC of Rs. 21,10,119/- traced to a Level-2 supplier whose registration was cancelled suo-motu w.e.f. 25.04.2025; and (c) alleged ineligible ITC of Rs. 3,17,979/- claimed to be not in furtherance of business. SECTION 3: Ground A – Rebuttal on Alleged Excess ITC of Rs. 3,51,274/- (GSTR-3B vs. GSTR-2B)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
<p>The allegation that ITC of Rs. 3,51,274/- pertaining to M/s Mahavir Communications (GSTIN 27AMAPB2309R1Z2) was 'rejected' in GSTR-2B and yet availed in GSTR-3B is factually incorrect and legally unsustainable. The Applicant has, in the ordinary course of business, received the tax invoice, received the goods/services, and made payment (including tax) to the said supplier as evidenced by the tax invoice, e-way bill, GRN, ledger extract and bank payment advices being annexed herewith as <strong>Annexure A-1 to A-5</strong>. Under Section 16(2), the substantive conditions for availment of ITC stand fully satisfied.</p><p>The mismatch, if any, arises from the fact that the said invoice appears in the supplier's GSTR-1 and is duly reflected in the Applicant's GSTR-2B; the reference to 'rejection' in the SCN pertains to the IMS (Invoice Management System) action, which does <em>not</em> extinguish substantive ITC entitlement where the supplier has discharged the tax. Reliance is placed on the settled principle laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in <em>Union of India v. Bharti Airtel Ltd. (2021) 131 taxmann.com 319 (SC)</em> and by the Hon'ble Madras High Court in <em>D.Y. Beathel Enterprises v. STO (2022) 63 GSTL 385</em>, that the recipient cannot be denied ITC where the supplier's tax has been paid to the exchequer. The Applicant undertakes to produce the supplier's GSTR-3B evidence of tax payment. Accordingly, the reversal proposed under Ground (a) is liable to be dropped.</p>

SECTION 4: Ground B – Rebuttal on Alleged Level-2 Cancelled Supplier ITC of Rs. 21,10,119/-
-----------------------------------------------------------------
<p>The allegation that ITC of Rs. 21,10,119/- (IGST) availed from M/s Nidimo International (GSTIN 27AAWFN1025A1ZI) — the Applicant's Level-1 supplier — is 'ineligible' merely because <em>its</em> upstream Level-2 supplier M/s Shree Shyam Mobility (GSTIN 24AFOFS7757K1Z5) was cancelled suo-motu w.e.f. 25.04.2025, is wholly untenable in law. The Applicant's direct supplier M/s Nidimo International is, and continues to be, an active registered person, and has duly issued tax invoices, effected supply, and reported the outward supplies in its GSTR-1 (reflected in the Applicant's GSTR-2B). All four cumulative conditions of Section 16(2) — possession of tax invoice, receipt of goods, tax paid to Government (Section 16(2)(c)), and filing of return — stand satisfied at the Applicant's end.</p><p>The GST law contains <em>no provision</em> requiring a recipient to verify the compliance status of its supplier's supplier (i.e., Level-2). The concept of 'Level-2 verification' is an internal departmental analytics exercise devoid of statutory backing. This precise question stands conclusively settled by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in <em>State of Karnataka v. Ecom Gill Coffee Trading Pvt. Ltd. (2023) 71 GSTL 129 (SC)</em>, and more directly under GST by the Hon'ble Calcutta High Court in <em>Suncraft Energy Pvt. Ltd. v. Assistant Commissioner (2023) 78 GSTL 424 (Cal)</em> (SLP dismissed by Hon'ble SC on 14.12.2023), and the Hon'ble Madras High Court in <em>D.Y. Beathel (supra)</em>, holding that ITC cannot be denied to a bona fide recipient on account of default/cancellation of an upstream supplier without first proceeding against the defaulting supplier. Further, CBIC Press Release dated 04.05.2018 and Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST dated 27.12.2022 support the position that recipients are not to be denied credit for supplier-side defects where their own compliance is complete. Accordingly, the proposed disallowance under Ground (b) is unsustainable.</p>

SECTION 6: Delinking of ITC-based Objections from Refund of IGST paid on Exports under Rule 96
-----------------------------------------------------------------
<p>Without prejudice to the above, it is respectfully submitted that the present refund is claimed under Rule 96 of the CGST Rules (refund of IGST paid on export of goods), where the shipping bill itself is deemed to be the refund application and refund is sanctioned on the basis of tax actually paid on the zero-rated supply. The refund under Rule 96 is <em>not</em> a refund of unutilised ITC under Rule 89(4). Therefore, alleged discrepancies in ITC availment (which, in any event, are contested above) cannot form the basis of rejection of IGST refund on exports, so long as: (a) the exports have been effected; (b) the IGST on the shipping bill has been paid; and (c) GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B for the period are duly filed. This position is supported by CBIC Circular No. 125/44/2019-GST dated 18.11.2019 and reiterated in Circular No. 197/09/2023-GST dated 17.07.2023.</p><p>The Hon'ble Gujarat High Court in <em>Amit Cotton Industries v. Principal Commissioner (2019) 29 GSTL 200 (Guj)</em> has categorically held that refund of IGST paid on exports cannot be withheld on collateral ITC-related objections. The Applicant respectfully invokes the ratio of the said decision.</p>
